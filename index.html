I.

Built Like the Mark

Every line of the hardware follows the Sanctum design — the same gold-on-black language carried on the XID-001 car and bike. Nothing chrome, nothing plastic-looking: black stone, cut gold, and the emblem doing the talking.

Gold on Black, Always

A matte black tower with hand-laid gold pinstripes and a gold-leaf wordmark. The finish matches the Sanctum cars and bikes, so the console, the rides, and the church all read as one family.

Cracked-Pillar Frame

The tower's shoulders step in like the tops of two pillars, and fine crack lines are etched down both front corners — the pillars from the emblem, built into the body of the machine itself.

Diamond Flowers

Golden diamond-shaped flowers run along the base plinth and ring the controller's home button — small, sharp blooms instead of soft petals, cut like gems into the black.

II.

Anatomy of the Emblem

The same mark from the car and the bike — the pendant itself: a diamond sword between two cracked stone pillars, hanging from a heavy gold Figaro chain. Enlarged and set into the console's face, it lights from behind when the machine is on, like a sign over a door.

1

The Chain

A heavy gold Figaro chain — long links, then short — with a diamond-set bail. The pendant hangs from it, and on the console the chain arcs over the top of the mark, worked into the tower's crown.

2

The Sword

A diamond-covered sword standing point-down between the pillars: gold pommel and crossguard up top, baguette diamonds running down the blade, the tip ending in the flowers. On the console it sits at chest height, the first thing you see when the machine wakes.

3

Two Cracked Pillars

A pair of stone pillars in pale silver-white, paved with small diamonds and each split by a jagged crack running down its face. They frame the sword and give the tower its stepped, temple-front shape.

4

The Flowers

A bunch of gold flowers gathered at the pillars' feet, around the sword's point — roses and lilies with diamond-shaped petals. They repeat along the console's base plinth and around the controller's sticks.

5

The Wordmark

SANCTUM in spaced gold capitals under the mark, with LIUGN CONSOLE beneath it in smaller type. The same lettering as the sign on the gothic church in Tab Atlanta.

III.

Top Vent & Crown

The crown of the tower is a stepped vent that breathes heat straight up, edged in a single gold line. It is shaped like the top step of a pillar — the machine wears its emblem even from above.

IV.

The Controller

Laid out the way Liugn — 𐌻𐌹𐌿𐌲𐌽 already plays: left stick to move, right stick to look and orbit the camera, face buttons for jump, interact, vehicle, and powers. Tap a marker to read each control.

Control 1 of 6

Left Stick — Move

Fast, GTA-paced movement: walk, push to run, click to sprint. The stick is ringed in gold with a deep-gold cap, tuned so the character never feels slow or stuttery on foot.

VI.

Design Targets

The numbers this design is specced to hit — a small, quiet box that boots straight into the game and runs it at a smooth 60 frames per second on a TV.

FORMVertical tower, 92 × 310 × 260 mm — stands like a pillar, or lays flat under a TV. Matte black stone-finish shell, gold pinstriping, etched crack lines.
DISPLAY OUT4K at 60 fps target for Liugn — 𐌻𐌹𐌿𐌲𐌽, 1080p high-refresh mode, HDR, one HDMI 2.1 port.
STORAGE1 TB internal SSD — the full game, every arcade title from the library, and room for creator content from LIUGN Cars Studio and Clothes Studio.
DISC DRIVEVertical slot-load drive in the Sanctum Edition — for a physical demo disc edition of the game. The slot glows gold when a disc is in.
CONTROLLERWireless, USB-C charging, ~30 hours per charge. Left stick move, right stick look / photo orbit, dedicated buttons for jump, interact, vehicle, and powers.
BOOTStraight into the game. Power on, the emblem lights, and Sanctum OS opens on the library — one press of A and you are standing in Tab Atlanta.
SOUND3D spatial audio over HDMI or the controller's 3.5 mm jack — thunder you can place in the room when HIM is searching.
IN THE BOXConsole, one Sanctum controller, HDMI cable, power cable, and a printed emblem plate for the tower's face.

This page is the design of the device — the look, the controller, and the home screen. A physical console is factory hardware; these are the targets a production version would be built and costed against.

VIII.

How a Console Like This Gets Made

The honest road from this design page to a box under a TV.

STEP 1

Design

This page: the device, the controller, the emblem, the finishes, and the home screen — the full Sanctum look, locked.

STEP 2

Prototype

A small single-board computer in a 3D-printed Sanctum shell, set to boot straight into the PC build of Liugn — 𐌻𐌹𐌿𐌲𐌽. Playable on a TV with the controller layout above.

STEP 3

Demo Disc

The playable demo path today is PC. A PlayStation disc needs Sony's partner program, a dev kit, and a console port — a bigger, paid step, taken when the game is ready for it.

STEP 4

Production

Injection-molded shells, real boards, safety certification, and factory runs. This is the step where target prices become real prices.
